# PARA Method

Tiago Forte's organizing scheme: **P**rojects, **A**reas, **R**esources, **A**rchive. The organizing question isn't "what topic is this?" but "how actionable is this?" — which is why it survives contact with real life better than topic trees.

- **Projects** — an outcome and an end date ("Ship the Q3 report")
- **Areas** — a standard to maintain with no end date (Health, Finances)
- **Resources** — reference material for someday
- **Archive** — anything inactive from the other three

## Why it matters to me

It's the skeleton of this vault: `10-Projects/`, `20-Areas/`, `30-Resources/`, `40-Archive/`, with `00-Inbox/` in front as the capture zone and `50-Wiki/` alongside for evergreen entities. The numeric prefixes just force a useful sort order.

The distinction that does the most work day to day: if it has a deadline it's a Project, if it's a forever-responsibility it's an Area. When a project ends, it moves to Archive rather than getting deleted — recall still works years later while the active folders stay small.
