# Maps of Content

Nick Milo's linking hubs (LYT). A MOC gathers scattered notes on a topic into one workbench where you can *think about* them — a thinking tool first, a navigation tool second.

*"MOCs are 'overlays' that add relevant information but that don't affect the base content"* — and, crucially, *"they are not hierarchical. They are heterarchical."* A note can appear in many MOCs, or none.

## When to build one: the mental squeeze point

**Not preemptively.** You build a MOC when scattered notes on a topic have become genuinely overwhelming — *"just like putting 20 index cards on an inviting rustic workbench."* Building empty MOC scaffolding before that point is the classic beginner error.

At this vault's current size, the honest expectation is **0–3 MOCs**, not fifteen.

## The three phases

1. **Assembling** — dump links on the workbench. No structure. Fast.
2. **Colliding** — *"use it like a crucible where ideas can battle it out for positioning."* Group and nest the links so their arrangement carries meaning. **This is the phase people skip**, and skipping it is why most MOCs are just ugly link lists.
3. **Unifying** — it becomes "a renewable summation of thought for the future you."

## MOC vs `_Index.md` — different species, keep both

| | `_Index.md` | MOC |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | One folder, 1:1 with the filesystem | A topic, across folders |
| Completeness | **Exhaustive** — every file | **Curated** — omission is the feature |
| Membership | A note is in exactly one | Zero, one, or many |
| Ordering | Mechanical | **Relational** — order carries meaning |
| Maintained by | Claude, automatically | You, with judgment |

The rule in `CLAUDE.md` that indexes must stay current is correct *for indexes* and wrong for MOCs.

## Keeping them from rotting

- **Curated top, dynamic bottom.** Hand-place only the links you can annotate; let a Bases/Dataview query catch the rest. Anything sitting in the query for months either needs promoting or genuinely is peripheral — which makes decay *visible*.
- **Queries create no backlinks.** That's what stops a MOC's forty links from flooding every target's backlink pane.
- **Split at the section level**: when a `##` section outgrows ~7–10 items, promote it to its own MOC and leave a single link behind.
