# Context vs Connections

[[Nate Herk]]'s filter for what belongs in a second brain.

- **Context** = evergreen business/life data. Who people are, what projects exist, decisions and why they were made, distilled reference material. Worth storing; still true in a year.
- **Connections** = transient traffic. Slack threads, one-off emails, raw logs, chat transcripts. High volume, low durability.

The failure mode is filling the brain with connections. It feels productive — look how much I captured — but it buries the context and poisons every future search with noise.

## Why it matters to me

This is the quality bar for every note Claude writes here, and the reason `/wrap` distills rather than transcribes. The working test: **would this matter in a month?** If not, it doesn't go in. A single well-linked paragraph about a person beats fifty saved messages mentioning them.

Corollary for capture: it's fine for raw material to hit `00-Inbox/` — that's a staging area, not the vault proper. It becomes context when it's processed, summarized, and linked; anything that never earns that treatment gets archived.
